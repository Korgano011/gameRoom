The Setup

• The Spoilers: Three panelists who all held doctorates sat inside isolation booths. They could not see the board or the game's secret subject.
• The Board: The champion was shown a secret subject (a famous person, place, or thing) and a board containing 8 numbered clues. (Note: Early pilots used 10 clues, but the broadcast series standardized to 8).

Gameplay Mechanics

The champion's ultimate goal was to give exactly 4 clues to the Spoilers without allowing them to correctly identify the subject.
1. Pick a Clue: The champion called out a number to reveal a clue. After reading it privately, they had to make a strategic choice:
	• GIVE: If the champion believed the clue was obscure enough to stump the academics, they chose to "give" it.
	• PASS: If the champion felt the clue was too easy or gave away too much, they could "pass". The champion had a maximum of 4 passes available.
2. The Spoilers' Guess: When a clue was given, the Spoilers were read the text and each given a brief moment to write down or state a guess.
3. Knocking Out Spoilers:
	• If a Spoiler guessed the subject correctly, they won $100 for themselves and were eliminated (retired) from the rest of the round.
	• If a Spoiler guessed incorrectly or failed to answer, the champion was awarded $100. That Spoiler remained active for the next clue.

Winning and Losing

• Winning the Grand Prize: If the champion successfully presented all 4 clues and at least one Spoiler remained active and stumped by the end, the champion won the grand prize of $5,000.
• Losing: If all three Spoilers managed to guess the subject correctly at any point during the round, the game ended immediately. The champion failed to get the $5,000 but got to keep whatever pocket money ($100 per wrong guess) they accumulated up to that point.
